# OD223 compute-leaves-field-unassigned

A compute method leaves a non-stored readonly field unassigned on some records.

| Category | Severity | Series | Needs the index |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Correctness | Error | saas~13.3 and later | yes |

## What it reports

A compute method with a path through its loop over `self` that ends an iteration without assigning a
field it computes, or that leaves the loop before the last record, when Odoo neither stores the
field nor lets users write it. Odoo merges every definition of the field in the module and its
`depends`, so the merged one decides: `store` is absent or false, `readonly` is true, or absent with
no `inverse`, and the field is not `related`. A module that Odoo always installs with `base`, such
as `web`, counts as in `depends`. An attribute that definitions set differently, or that `**kwargs`
may set, leaves the field unchecked. The finding is at the method's name, and names each such field.

The method must be in the class that defines the field and hold every assignment of the field in
its one `for record in self:` loop, each on that record. A method that calls `super()`, reads the
field, may assign it by a name built at runtime (`setattr`, a key that is not a literal, or
`update()` or `write()` given something other than a dict of literal keys), or calls a method of
`self` or passes `self` on outside the loop is not checked, nor is a method that a class of the
same model in the same module overrides by a method that calls it through `super()`, in any file,
since the override may assign the rest; an override in another module does not count. Through the
loop body:

- an assignment, `update()` or `write()` of the field, a `raise`, and a call of a method of the
  record or of `self`, or a call given the record, which may assign the field in a helper, count as
  assigning it;
- a `continue` or `break` before that is reported, and so is an `if` without `else` whose test is
  false, but a chain with an `elif` and no `else`, like a `match`, is taken to cover every case;
- an `if` with no `elif` assigns the field when its test holds if its body assigns it, or when the
  test does not hold if its lone `else` does, and a `for` loop whose body assigns it does when its
  iterable is not empty; two such statements of the same block cover every path only when one
  assigns exactly when the other does not: `if x:` and `if not x:`, `if x:` and another `if x:` that
  assigns in its `else`, `for line in lines:` and `if not lines:`, or one comparison of the same
  operands under opposite operators, such as `==` and `!=`, with the operands of `==`, `!=`, `is`
  and `is not` in either order; two `if` statements that assign under the same test are reported,
  and so are `== "a"` and `== "b"`, and two loops that each assign the field;
- such a statement in the body of a `with` pairs with a sibling statement of the enclosing block,
  unless the `with` runs under `contextlib.suppress`, and so does one in the body or `else` of a
  `try` when every handler assigns the field or raises, since an exception may skip the
  assignment; in the body of an `if x:`, a statement that assigns when `x` holds, such as
  `for line in x:`, covers every path, and so in a lone `else` does one that assigns when `x` does
  not;
- a `continue` of a nested loop before the assignment means that loop may assign nothing, so after
  `for line in lines: if not line.x: continue` then `record.f = line.x`, that loop does not pair
  with `if not lines:`;
- a `return` in the loop body is reported, before or after the assignment, since the records after
  it stay unassigned, and so is a `break` after the assignment that leaves the loop over `self`;
  neither is when it is in a nested function, or follows, as a statement of its block or of a
  block around it, a call of a method of `self`, which may assign the field on every record, a
  `raise`, or a statement that makes one of them on every path: a `with` whose body does, unless
  it runs under `contextlib.suppress`, an `if` with an `elif` or `else` or a `match` each of whose
  branches does, a `while True:` loop whose body does before every `break` of it, or a `try` whose
  body or `else` does and so does each handler, or whose `finally` does; a `return` or `break` in
  a `finally` is still reported when a `continue` or `raise` after the assignment skips that call;
- a `try` assigns when its body and `else` do and so does each handler, or when its `finally` does,
  and a `return` in any of them, or a `break` that leaves the loop over `self`, is reported, even
  before a `finally` that assigns, unless that `finally` calls a method of `self`; a handler finds
  the field assigned when the `try` body, after nothing but `pass` and constants bound to names,
  first assigns the field alone (`record.f = ...`, not chained or unpacked) `False` or `None`,
  another falsy constant unless it is a `One2many` or `Many2many` field, or a number, negative too,
  to an `Integer`, `Float`, `Monetary` or `Many2one` field, since Odoo refuses any other value of
  some type, such as a `Selection` value outside its list;
- a nested loop may run no iteration, so its `else` counts, and only when every `break` in its body
  assigns first; a `while True:` loop counts when its body assigns before every `break`, and a
  `continue` in it only starts the next iteration, so the paths to its `break` decide; when a
  `break` may come before the first statement that calls a method of `self` or raises, on every
  path, a body that may `continue` still counts as assigning but not as that call, so a later
  `return` or `break` after an assignment is reported.

## Why it matters

When code reads a computed field, Odoo calls its method on the records to compute, then looks for
those it left without a value. Odoo raises `ValueError: Compute method failed to assign` for a
field it neither stores nor lets users write, so the form, list or report that reads the field
fails for the records the method skipped, often only for some data. Other computed fields get
`False` silently. Assign the field on every path of the loop, with `False` when there is no value.

Odoo raises that error from saas~13.3: it came to saas~13.5 and 14.0 with 126ebbc905af, and was
backported to saas~13.3 with a555b71261f6 and saas~13.4 with ec12ff214ddf. From 13.0 to saas~13.2,
a field the method leaves unassigned raises `CacheMiss`, a `KeyError`, when Odoo does not store it
or the record is new, whether users can write it or not. 12.0 sets every field the method computes
to `False` before calling it, so nothing raises.

The rule does not run for a module when the module or one of its dependencies depends on a module
that is not indexed. A model is skipped when the class that introduces it, with `_name` or as a
class Odoo names, is in a module outside the dependencies, or when a class of it, or of a model it
inherits or delegates to, has a plain Python base class, whose fields only Python knows.

## Example

```python title="models/res_partner.py"
class ResPartner(models.Model):
    _inherit = "res.partner"

    initials = fields.Char(compute="_compute_initials")

    @api.depends("name")
    def _compute_initials(self):
        for partner in self:
            if partner.name:
                partner.initials = "".join(word[0] for word in partner.name.split())
```

A partner without a name, such as a new address in a form, makes reading `initials` raise.

```python title="models/res_partner.py"
class ResPartner(models.Model):
    _inherit = "res.partner"

    initials = fields.Char(compute="_compute_initials")

    @api.depends("name")
    def _compute_initials(self):
        for partner in self:
            if partner.name:
                partner.initials = "".join(word[0] for word in partner.name.split())
            else:
                partner.initials = False
```

## Suppressing

```python
def _compute_initials(self):  # odooscan: ignore[OD223]
```
